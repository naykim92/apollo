Stress-Pattern Matching Experiment

Listen to a novel spoken word and choose the English word with the most similar stress pattern.

In this study, you will hear short nonsense (gibberish) words. These sounds are not real English words and do not have meanings. Your task is to listen to the stress pattern of each sound and decide which English word has the most similar stress pattern.

What is a stress pattern?

In a multi-syllable word, one syllable is often heard as more prominent than the others. For this task, focus on where the main stress falls. You do not need to identify the gibberish word or guess what it means.

Concrete example

First, listen to two familiar English words. They contain the same number of syllables, but the main stress falls in different positions.

Your task on each trial

  1. Play the gibberish audio and listen for where the strongest emphasis falls.
  2. Read each of the four English options as you would normally pronounce it.
  3. As you read each option, notice which syllable naturally receives the main stress.
  4. Compare that stress pattern with the one you heard in the gibberish audio.
  5. Choose the English word whose pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables is the closest match.

Important: Do not choose a word because its letters or individual sounds resemble the gibberish, and do not use the word's meaning. Instead, mentally pronounce each option in your usual way and pay attention to which syllable you naturally emphasize. Then compare that pattern of stronger and weaker syllables with the pattern you heard in the audio.

Before you begin

  • Please use headphones or earphones if possible.
  • Complete the task in a quiet environment.
  • You may replay an audio clip, but please rely on your first clear judgment rather than overthinking.
  • This is not a vocabulary test.

Practice

Before the main task, review two examples. Each example contains a real English word and a gibberish recording produced with a matching stress pattern. These examples demonstrate what “same stress pattern” means.

Now try one practice question. This item is not scored. Listen to the gibberish audio first. Then read each English option in your normal pronunciation, notice which syllable receives the main stress, and choose the option whose stress pattern best matches the audio.
